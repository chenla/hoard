Notes

October 1927. Lorentz is 74. He has chaired every Solvay Conference since the first in 1911. He won the Nobel Prize twenty-five years ago. His Lorentz transformations became the mathematical foundation of Einstein's special relativity.

He will be dead in four months.

This is his last conference, though no one knows it. He presides over the Bohr-Einstein debates with the authority of a man who has watched physics transform from classical certainty to quantum uncertainty across his lifetime. He is respected by everyone in the room — perhaps the only person both Einstein and Bohr would defer to.

When he dies in February 1928, Einstein and Curie attend his funeral. Ehrenfest succeeds him at Leiden. Langevin succeeds him as president of the Solvay conferences. The photograph from this conference becomes, in retrospect, a portrait of the last time the old guard and the new sat together under his chairmanship.
