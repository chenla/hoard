Notes

Wolfgang Pauli (1900–1958). Austrian-Swiss theoretical physicist. Nobel Prize in Physics 1945 for the discovery of the exclusion principle (1925), which states that no two fermions can occupy the same quantum state simultaneously — a cornerstone of atomic structure and the periodic table.

Predicted the existence of the neutrino in 1930 to explain the continuous energy spectrum of beta decay. Godson of Ernst Mach. Feared throughout physics for his withering criticism — his dismissal "not even wrong" became legendary. At 27 he was one of the younger participants at the 1927 Solvay Conference, already recognized as one of the sharpest minds in theoretical physics.
