** Scope Note

German mathematician (1862–1943).  Posed the 23 problems
that shaped twentieth-century mathematics.  His
formalization program — the attempt to ground all
mathematics on a complete, consistent, decidable
foundation — was the mathematical equivalent of the
deterministic worldview.  Gödel destroyed it in 1931.

** Notes

David Hilbert (1862–1943).  Professor at the University
of Göttingen, which under his leadership became the
world center for mathematics.  His 1900 address to the
International Congress of Mathematicians posed 23
problems that set the research agenda for the century —
several remain unsolved.

His program to formalize mathematics (1920s) was the
last great attempt to save the deterministic vision in
the mathematical domain.  If successful, every
mathematical question would have a definitive answer
derivable from axioms.  Von Neumann was among his most
brilliant champions.  Gödel's incompleteness theorems
(1931) showed all three goals — completeness,
consistency, decidability — were unachievable
simultaneously.

His epitaph reads "Wir müssen wissen.  Wir werden
wissen." (We must know.  We shall know.) — spoken in a
radio address just days before Gödel presented his
incompleteness theorems at the Königsberg conference.
The timing is almost unbearable: the most confident
assertion of the deterministic program, delivered at the
precise moment of its destruction.
