** Notes

Emile Henriot (1885–1961).  French physical chemist.
Professor at the University of Paris.  Demonstrated
the natural radioactivity of potassium and rubidium,
showing that radioactivity was not confined to heavy
elements.

Pioneer of ultracentrifuge technology, developing
air-turbine-driven centrifuges capable of extremely
high rotational speeds — tools that would later prove
essential in biochemistry and isotope separation.

At the 1927 Solvay Conference he served as one of
the two scientific secretaries alongside Auguste
Piccard, handling the documentation and organizational
work that made the proceedings possible.  The
scientific secretaries occupied an unusual position —
junior enough to serve an administrative function,
but accomplished enough to follow the discussions.
His experimental work, while less famous than that
of the quantum theorists present, was valued for its
precision and practical ingenuity.
