** Notes

Paul Adrien Maurice Dirac (1902–1984).  English
theoretical physicist.  Nobel Prize in Physics 1933
(shared with Schrödinger, for new forms of atomic
theory).  Predicted the existence of antimatter (1928).
The Dirac equation unified quantum mechanics and special
relativity.

Famously laconic — colleagues joked that a "dirac" was
the unit of one word per hour.  Lucasian Professor of
Mathematics at Cambridge (1932–1969), the chair once
held by Newton.

Dirac arrived at the 1927 Solvay Conference as one of
its youngest participants and said almost nothing during
the debates.  He did not need to.  His 1925 paper on
the general theory of quantum mechanics had already
provided a more elegant formulation than either
Heisenberg's matrix mechanics or Schrödinger's wave
equation alone.  His transformation theory (1927)
showed that the two approaches were mathematically
equivalent — different representations of the same
underlying structure.  Where others debated philosophy,
Dirac simply wrote down the mathematics.

Within a year of Solvay, he published the Dirac
equation (1928), which unified quantum mechanics with
special relativity and predicted the existence of
antimatter — a particle identical to the electron but
with opposite charge.  When the positron was discovered
by Carl Anderson in 1932, it confirmed one of the most
extraordinary theoretical predictions in the history of
physics: Dirac had deduced the existence of a new form
of matter from pure mathematics.  His textbook /The
Principles of Quantum Mechanics/ (1930) became the
standard reference for generations of physicists, and
his notation — bra-ket notation — remains the standard
language of quantum mechanics.

His later work on magnetic monopoles, the Dirac sea
(an infinite sea of negative-energy electrons whose
"holes" appear as positrons), and quantum field theory
continued to shape physics for decades.  He spent his
final years at Florida State University, largely
forgotten by the broader public but revered by
physicists as perhaps the purest mathematical talent of
the twentieth century.

*** Key Works

**** Before Solvay

- "The Fundamental Equations of Quantum Mechanics"
  (1925).  An independent formulation of quantum
  mechanics, more general than either Heisenberg's or
  Schrödinger's.
- Transformation theory (1927).  Proved that matrix
  mechanics and wave mechanics are mathematically
  equivalent — different representations of one theory.

**** After Solvay

- The Dirac equation (1928).  Unified quantum mechanics
  with special relativity and predicted the existence
  of antimatter.
- /The Principles of Quantum Mechanics/ (1930).  The
  standard textbook — introduced bra-ket notation and
  shaped how quantum mechanics was taught and practiced
  for generations.
- Dirac sea (1930).  The theoretical framework that
  predicted positrons as "holes" in an infinite sea
  of negative-energy electrons.
- Magnetic monopole prediction (1931).  Showed that
  the existence of a single magnetic monopole would
  explain the quantization of electric charge.
