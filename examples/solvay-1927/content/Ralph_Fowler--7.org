** Notes

Ralph Fowler (1889–1944).  British physicist and
mathematician.  Fellow of the Royal Society and
professor at Cambridge.  Made important contributions
to statistical mechanics and astrophysics, including
work on stellar spectra and the thermodynamics of
white dwarf stars.

Co-formulated the zeroth law of thermodynamics with
Edward Guggenheim.  His most lasting influence was as
a mentor — he was Dirac's doctoral supervisor and
introduced him to quantum theory, arguably the single
most consequential act of mentorship in 20th-century
physics.  Son-in-law of Ernest Rutherford, he served
as a bridge between the experimental and theoretical
traditions at Cambridge.

Trained a generation of British theoretical
physicists at the Cavendish Laboratory, including
Chandrasekhar, Lennard-Jones, and Mott.  Died young
at 55 during World War II, his later years spent on
ballistics and ordnance research for the British
military.

*** Key Works

**** Before Solvay

- /Statistical Mechanics/ (1929, published shortly
  after Solvay).  Textbook that trained a generation
  of British physicists in the new methods.

**** After Solvay

- /Fowler-Nordheim tunneling/ (1928).  With
  Nordheim, described quantum tunneling of electrons
  through potential barriers — essential to modern
  semiconductor physics.

- /Stellar collapse theory/ (1926).  Applied
  Fermi-Dirac statistics to white dwarf stars,
  showing that electron degeneracy pressure prevents
  further collapse.
