** Notes

Paul Ehrenfest (1880–1933).  Austrian-Dutch
theoretical physicist.  Succeeded Lorentz as
professor of theoretical physics at Leiden in 1912.
Known for the Ehrenfest theorem, which bridges
classical and quantum mechanics, and for the
Ehrenfest classification of phase transitions.

Legendary for his penetrating questions and his
ability to expose the core of any argument.  A close
friend of both Einstein and Bohr, he played the role
of honest broker in their debates at the 1927 Solvay
Conference.  Struggled with depression throughout his
life and died by suicide in September 1933, a loss
deeply felt across the physics community.

Ehrenfest's role at Solvay was unique.  He was not one
of the architects of quantum theory, nor one of its
critics — he was its most rigorous interrogator.  When
Bohr's arguments became opaque, Ehrenfest demanded
clarity.  When Einstein's thought experiments were
dismissed too quickly, Ehrenfest insisted they be taken
seriously.  He understood both positions from the inside,
which made him indispensable and also, increasingly,
unbearable to himself.  In a letter to his closest
friends, he described feeling intellectually paralyzed
— unable to keep up with the pace of discovery, unable
to choose a side.

His adiabatic principle — the idea that certain
quantities remain invariant under slow changes to a
system — was a key bridge concept between classical and
quantum physics, connecting Boltzmann's statistical
mechanics to the new quantum theory.  His classification
of phase transitions remains a standard framework in
thermodynamics.

On September 25, 1933, Ehrenfest shot his son Wassik,
who had Down syndrome, in the waiting room of the
Vossius Institute in Amsterdam, then killed himself.
He had been consumed by depression, professional
self-doubt, and despair over the political situation in
Europe.  Einstein wrote to the widow of their mutual
friend: "I have not been able to believe it.  I found
it the most incomprehensible thing I have ever
encountered."  The human cost of living at the boundary
between paradigms.

*** Key Works

**** Before Solvay

- /Begriffliche Grundlagen der statistischen Auffassung
  in der Mechanik/ (1912).  Encyclopedic review of
  statistical mechanics, co-written with Tatiana
  Ehrenfest-Afanassjewa, that clarified Boltzmann's
  legacy.
- Adiabatic hypothesis (1913–1916).  Principle
  connecting classical mechanics to quantum conditions
  via slowly varying systems.
- Ehrenfest theorem (1927).  Proved that quantum
  expectation values obey classical equations of motion.

**** After Solvay

- Classification of phase transitions (1933).  Standard
  taxonomy distinguishing first-order and continuous
  phase transitions, still used today.
- Contributions to statistical mechanics (1927–1933).
  Continued work on foundational problems in
  thermodynamics and quantum statistics until his
  death.
