** Notes

October 1927.  Dirac is 25, the same age as Heisenberg.
He completed his PhD at Cambridge just one year ago.  His
transformation theory, published earlier this year,
provided the first unified mathematical framework
connecting Heisenberg's matrix mechanics and
Schrödinger's wave mechanics — showing they were the
same theory in different mathematical clothing.

He says almost nothing at the conference.  This is
characteristic.  While Einstein and Bohr debate
philosophy, Dirac works on equations.  Heisenberg will
later recall that during a dinner discussion about
religion and physics, Bohr made subtle arguments, Einstein
pushed back — and Dirac said flatly that he did not see
the need for the hypothesis of God.

Within a year he will publish the Dirac equation,
unifying quantum mechanics with special relativity and
predicting the existence of antimatter — a prediction
confirmed four years later.  He will share the Nobel Prize
with Schrödinger in 1933, at age 31.

In the photograph he stands in the middle row, looking
slightly elsewhere, as if the social occasion around him
is a minor inconvenience interrupting something more
important happening inside his head.
