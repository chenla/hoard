** Notes

Paul Langevin (1872–1946).  French physicist.  Known
for his theories of paramagnetism and diamagnetism
(the Langevin equation) and for pioneering ultrasonic
submarine detection during World War I using
piezoelectric quartz.

A student of Pierre Curie at the Sorbonne, he
maintained a lifelong connection to the Curie circle.
He succeeded Lorentz as president of the Solvay
Conference after Lorentz's death in 1928.  An outspoken
anti-fascist, he was placed under house arrest during
the German occupation of France.
