** Notes

Paul Langevin (1872–1946).  French physicist.  Known
for his theories of paramagnetism and diamagnetism
(the Langevin equation) and for pioneering ultrasonic
submarine detection during World War I using
piezoelectric quartz.

A student of Pierre Curie at the Sorbonne, he
maintained a lifelong connection to the Curie circle.
He succeeded Lorentz as president of the Solvay
Conference after Lorentz's death in 1928, serving as
the institutional bridge between the pre-war
generation of physicists and the quantum
revolutionaries.

An outspoken anti-fascist and member of the French
Communist Party, he was placed under house arrest
during the German occupation of France.  His
political courage matched his scientific standing —
he used his position to protest the persecution of
Jewish colleagues and the suppression of academic
freedom.  He died in 1946, shortly after the
liberation.

*** Key Works

**** Before Solvay

- /Langevin equation for paramagnetism/ (1905).
  Statistical treatment of magnetic dipoles that
  explained paramagnetic behavior.

- /Ultrasonic submarine detection/ (1917).  Pioneered
  sonar using piezoelectric quartz during WWI.

**** After Solvay

- /President, Solvay Conferences/ (1928–1946).
  Succeeded Lorentz; guided the conferences through
  the interwar period.
