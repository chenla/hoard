** Notes

John Stewart Bell (1928–1990).  Grew up in working-class
Belfast, the first in his family to attend university.
Studied at Queen's University Belfast, then moved to
CERN in Geneva where he spent his career designing
particle accelerators — his "day job."

His theoretical work on quantum foundations was done
largely in his spare time, a fact that says as much
about the culture of physics as about Bell himself.
Questioning the foundations of quantum mechanics was
considered eccentric at best; the prevailing attitude
was "shut up and calculate."  Bell calculated — and
his calculations showed that the question mattered.

His 1964 paper "On the Einstein Podolsky Rosen Paradox"
is one of the most cited papers in the history of
physics.  It proved that the EPR debate was not merely
philosophical — it made experimentally testable
predictions.  He died suddenly of a cerebral hemorrhage
on October 1, 1990, the year before the first
loophole-free Bell tests were attempted.  He was widely
tipped for the Nobel Prize.
