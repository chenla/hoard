** Notes

Owen Richardson (1879–1959).  British physicist.  Nobel
Prize in Physics 1928 for his work on thermionic
emission — the discharge of electrons from heated
surfaces.  His research established the Richardson
equation (sometimes called the Richardson-Dushman
equation), which describes the relationship between
emission current and temperature.

Professor at King's College London and later at
Princeton.  His work on thermionic emission laid the
foundation for vacuum tube electronics, which
dominated electrical engineering until the transistor
era.  Every radio, early computer, and amplifier
before the late 1950s depended on the physics
Richardson had characterized.

The Richardson equation remains relevant wherever
electron emission from hot surfaces matters — in
cathode-ray tubes, electron microscopes, and
thermionic energy converters.  His Nobel came in
1928, the year after Solvay, making him one of
several attendees honored in quick succession.

*** Key Works

**** Before Solvay

- /The Emission of Electricity from Hot Bodies/

**** After Solvay

  Later work on molecular spectroscopy at King's
