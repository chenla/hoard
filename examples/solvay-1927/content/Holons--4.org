** Scope Note

The composition unit in Hoard: a bounded scope that
groups cards into a publishable, navigable whole.
A holon defines its membership (which cards belong),
its expression preference (which version of shared
cards to show), and its ordering (how to arrange
them).  Holons are Koestler's holons made concrete —
simultaneously autonomous wholes and parts of a
larger hord.

** Notes

A holon is itself a card (type wh:holon) with special
sections: Membership, Expression, Order, and
optionally Primary (the anchor article).

Membership can be declared by tag (all cards tagged
~solvay-1927), by explicit filename, or both.  This
allows holons to grow organically as cards are tagged
during research, while also including cards that don't
carry the tag.

Expression preference is what makes holons powerful.
The same Einstein card can appear in multiple holons,
but each holon can request a different expression:
the Solvay holon wants Einstein-as-debater-in-1927;
a future holon about the Manhattan Project would want
Einstein-as-refugee-writing-to-Roosevelt.  The Whole
card is always the fallback — the full biography,
the complete concept.

Ordering determines how cards are arranged within the
holon: by type first (events, then people, then
concepts), then alphabetically within type.

Post holons have a Primary — an article card that
serves as the narrative anchor.  The context cloud
(margin cards, sidenotes) surrounds the primary.
Concept keystones like Scaffolds have no primary —
they are reference collections, not narratives.

Holons export to standalone sites: an index page,
individual card pages, and optionally a context-cloud
article view with Tufte-style sidenotes.  Each holon
is deployable independently.
