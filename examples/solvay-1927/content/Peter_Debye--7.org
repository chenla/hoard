** Notes

Peter Debye (1884–1966).  Dutch-American physicist and
physical chemist.  Nobel Prize in Chemistry 1936 for
contributions to the study of molecular structure
through dipole moments and X-ray diffraction.

Developed the Debye model of specific heat in solids
(extending Einstein's earlier model) and the
Debye-Huckel theory of electrolyte solutions, which
described how ions interact in solution — foundational
for all of electrochemistry.  Born in Maastricht, he
held professorships across Europe before emigrating
to the United States in 1940 to chair the chemistry
department at Cornell University.

His wartime role remains controversial.  As director
of the Kaiser Wilhelm Institute for Physics in Berlin,
he was asked by the Nazi government to take German
citizenship; he chose to leave for the United States
instead.  Later allegations that he had cooperated
with the regime by signing a letter expelling Jewish
members from the German Physical Society have been
debated by historians, with no clear consensus.

*** Key Works

**** Before Solvay

- /Debye model of specific heat/ (1912).  Extended
  Einstein's model to account for the full spectrum
  of lattice vibrations in solids.

- /Debye-Huckel theory/ (1923).  Described the
  behavior of electrolyte solutions — still the
  starting point for electrochemistry.

**** After Solvay

- /X-ray and electron diffraction studies/ (1930s).
  Work on molecular structure through scattering
  methods — the basis of his 1936 Nobel Prize.
