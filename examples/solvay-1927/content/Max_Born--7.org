** Notes

Max Born (1882–1970).  German-born British physicist.
Nobel Prize in Physics 1954 for the probabilistic
interpretation of the wave function — the Born rule,
which states that the square of the wave function's
amplitude gives the probability of finding a particle
at a given position.

At Gottingen he developed matrix mechanics together
with Heisenberg and Pascual Jordan, providing the
first rigorous mathematical formulation of quantum
mechanics.  Fled Germany in 1933 and settled in
Edinburgh.  His probabilistic interpretation was one
of the central ideas debated at the 1927 Solvay
Conference, where Einstein famously objected that
God does not play dice.
