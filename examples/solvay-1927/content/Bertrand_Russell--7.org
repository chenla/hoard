** Scope Note

British philosopher, logician, and mathematician
(1872–1970).  Nobel Prize in Literature 1950.
Co-authored /Principia Mathematica/ with Whitehead — the
most ambitious attempt to ground mathematics in pure
logic.  Russell's paradox had exposed the cracks;
/Principia/ was the repair job; Gödel showed the cracks
were structural.

** Notes

Bertrand Arthur William Russell (1872–1970).  Third Earl
Russell.  Discovered Russell's paradox in 1901 — the set
of all sets that do not contain themselves — which
showed that naive set theory was inconsistent.  This was
not an obscure edge case; it struck at the foundations
of mathematics itself.

He spent the next decade with Alfred North Whitehead
building /Principia Mathematica/ to repair the damage.
The three volumes (1910–1913) attempted to derive all
mathematical truths from pure logic, constructing an
elaborate type hierarchy to avoid the paradoxes.  It
was the most ambitious formalization project ever
attempted — and it assumed that such formalization was
possible.  Gödel showed it was not.

Russell was also a major figure in analytic philosophy,
a prolific popular writer, and a political activist.
He was imprisoned for pacifism during WWI, helped found
the Campaign for Nuclear Disarmament, and remained
publicly active into his nineties.
