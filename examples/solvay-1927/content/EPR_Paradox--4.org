** Scope Note

The Einstein-Podolsky-Rosen thought experiment (1935).
Argued that quantum mechanics must be incomplete because
it implied instantaneous correlations between distant
particles — "spooky action at a distance."  Einstein's
most sophisticated objection to Copenhagen.

** Notes

Published as "Can Quantum-Mechanical Description of
Physical Reality Be Considered Complete?" in /Physical
Review/ 47 (1935).  The paper defined "elements of
reality" and argued that if a physical quantity can be
predicted with certainty without disturbing the system,
it must correspond to something real — an element of
physical reality that quantum mechanics fails to
describe.

Bohr responded within months, rejecting the premise.
His reply argued that the EPR criterion of reality was
inapplicable to quantum systems — that the experimental
arrangement itself defines what can meaningfully be said
about the system.  The debate appeared purely
philosophical for nearly thirty years.

Then John Bell showed in 1964 that the question was
experimentally testable.  His theorem derived an
inequality that any local hidden-variable theory must
satisfy; quantum mechanics predicts violations.  Alain
Aspect's experiments (1982) confirmed quantum mechanics
and violated Bell's inequality — Einstein was wrong
about locality, but right that something profound was at
stake.  The EPR paper remains one of the most cited in
the history of physics.
