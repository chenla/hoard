** Scope Note

French physicist (1892–1987). Nobel Prize 1929. Proposed wave-particle duality of matter — the idea that particles have wavelengths. Presented pilot-wave theory at Solvay as a deterministic alternative to Copenhagen and watched Pauli demolish it. The 7th Duc de Broglie.

** Notes

Louis de Broglie (1892–1987).  French physicist.
Nobel Prize in Physics 1929 for his discovery of the
wave nature of electrons.  In his 1924 doctoral thesis
he proposed that all matter has wave-like properties,
with wavelength inversely proportional to momentum —
the de Broglie relation.

His idea was initially met with skepticism but was
championed by Einstein and experimentally confirmed
by Davisson and Germer in 1927.  It inspired
Schrödinger's development of wave mechanics.  The
7th Duc de Broglie, he came from one of France's
oldest noble families.  At the Solvay Conference he
presented his pilot-wave theory, which was criticized
and lay dormant for decades.

The Solvay Conference was the pivotal moment of de
Broglie's career, and not in the way he hoped.  He
presented his pilot-wave theory — a deterministic
interpretation in which particles are guided by real
waves — as an alternative to the emerging Copenhagen
consensus.  Wolfgang Pauli attacked it with a specific
counterexample that de Broglie could not answer on the
spot.  The theory was declared dead.  De Broglie
abandoned it and accepted the Copenhagen interpretation
for the next twenty-five years.

What makes this episode significant is what happened
later.  In 1952, David Bohm independently rediscovered
and extended the pilot-wave approach, showing that
Pauli's objection was not fatal.  De Broglie, then in
his sixties, returned to his original theory and spent
his remaining decades advocating for it.  The idea that
was killed at Solvay turned out to be viable — it had
simply been killed prematurely, by social pressure as
much as by physics.

De Broglie's aristocratic background was unusual in
twentieth-century physics.  He came to physics through
his older brother Maurice, an experimental physicist
who introduced him to the proceedings of the first
Solvay Conference.  His 1924 doctoral thesis — just
over a hundred pages — was so radical that the
examining committee sent it to Einstein for evaluation
before accepting it.  Einstein's endorsement made the
difference.  Five years later, de Broglie received the
Nobel Prize.

*** Key Works

**** Before Solvay

- /Recherches sur la théorie des quanta/ (1924).
  Doctoral thesis proposing wave-particle duality of
  matter — the de Broglie relation.
- Pilot-wave theory (1927).  Deterministic
  interpretation of quantum mechanics presented at the
  fifth Solvay Conference.

**** After Solvay

- Nobel Prize in Physics (1929).  For the discovery
  of the wave nature of electrons.
- Return to pilot-wave theory (1950s).  After Bohm's
  1952 papers, de Broglie revived and extended his
  original deterministic interpretation.
- /The Current Interpretation of Wave Mechanics/ (1964).
  Critique of the Copenhagen interpretation and defense
  of a causal, deterministic alternative.
