** Scope Note

Christopher Alexander's method for capturing design
knowledge as a network of named solutions to recurring
problems, each pattern connected to larger and smaller
patterns in a generative sequence.  A pattern language
is not a catalog but a grammar — the patterns combine
to produce coherent wholes that no single pattern could
achieve alone.

** Notes

Alexander published /A Pattern Language/ (1977) as
253 patterns ranging from the scale of regions (pattern
1: Independent Regions) down to construction details
(pattern 253: Things from Your Life).  Each pattern
has the same structure: a name, a problem statement,
the forces in tension, and a solution that resolves
them.  Patterns reference other patterns, forming a
network.

The deeper work is /The Timeless Way of Building/
(1979), which describes the "quality without a name"
— the property of places and things that feel alive,
whole, comfortable, free, exact, egoless, eternal.
Alexander argued this quality arises from pattern
languages used by the people who inhabit a place, not
by professionals designing for them.

Software adopted the term through the Gang of Four's
/Design Patterns/ (1994), but largely stripped out
Alexander's most radical claims: that patterns are
generative (they produce the design, not merely
describe it), that they must be used by inhabitants
rather than experts, and that the quality they produce
is objective, not subjective.

Alexander's later work, /The Nature of Order/ (2002–
2005), pushed further: fifteen fundamental properties
of living structure, the idea that matter itself
has degrees of life, and the claim that the process
of creation matters as much as the product.  This is
where Alexander most directly anticipates Hoard's
homoiconicity@@margin:Homoiconicity@@ — the structure of the thing and the
process that made it are not separable.

The unfinished problem in Alexander's work: pattern
languages require a community of practice to function,
but he never solved how to bootstrap that community
or how to scale pattern-based production beyond
individual buildings.@@margin:Shokunin@@  This is exactly the gap
Dispersal addresses.
