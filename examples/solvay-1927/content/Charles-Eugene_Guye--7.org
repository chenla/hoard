** Scope Note

Swiss experimental physicist whose precision measurements of mass variation with velocity provided early confirmation of special relativity. Einstein's teacher at the Zurich Polytechnic.

** Notes

Charles-Eugène Guye (1866–1942).  Swiss experimental
physicist.  Professor at the University of Geneva,
where he conducted precision experiments on the
variation of mass with velocity — among the earliest
experimental confirmations of relativistic mechanics.

Einstein was his student at the Zurich Polytechnic
(ETH).  Guye's experiments on the deflection of
high-speed electrons by electric and magnetic fields
provided some of the earliest quantitative
confirmation that mass increases with velocity as
special relativity predicts.  The measurements were
modest in scope but important — at the time few
experiments could test relativity directly.

He was one of the lesser-known but respected
experimentalists at the 1927 Solvay Conference, a
representative of the careful Swiss tradition of
precision measurement.  His role was that of the
quiet contributor whose work others built upon.

*** Key Works

**** Before Solvay

- /Comptes Rendus papers on relativistic mass/
  (1907–1921).  Series of precision experiments
  measuring electron mass variation with velocity,
  confirming special relativity predictions.

**** After Solvay

- /L'Evolution Physico-Chimique/ (1930s).  Later
  work applying physical methods to chemical
  problems at Geneva.
