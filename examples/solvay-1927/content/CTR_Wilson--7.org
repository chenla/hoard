** Notes

C.T.R. Wilson (1869–1959).  Scottish physicist.  Nobel
Prize in Physics 1927 (shared with Arthur Compton).
Inventor of the cloud chamber, a device that made the
tracks of charged particles visible for the first time.

Originally inspired by atmospheric phenomena observed
on Ben Nevis, he developed the expansion cloud chamber
at the Cavendish Laboratory in Cambridge.  The device
became an essential tool of particle physics for
decades, enabling the discovery of the positron and
the muon.
