** Scope Note

Australian-born British physicist. Nobel Prize 1915 at age 25 — still the youngest Physics laureate. Formulated Bragg's law for X-ray diffraction and founded X-ray crystallography. Later directed the Cavendish Laboratory, overseeing the work that led to the discovery of DNA's structure.

** Notes

William Lawrence Bragg (1890–1971).  Australian-born
British physicist.  Nobel Prize in Physics 1915
(shared with his father William Henry Bragg) for
their work on X-ray crystallography — at age 25 he
remains the youngest Nobel laureate in Physics.

Formulated Bragg's law (n*lambda = 2d*sin(theta)),
which describes the angles at which X-rays are
diffracted by crystal lattice planes — the equation
that made crystallography a quantitative science.
Founded the field of X-ray crystallography as a
practical tool for determining molecular structure,
transforming chemistry and biology.

Later directed the Cavendish Laboratory at Cambridge,
where he created the environment that allowed Watson
and Crick to discover the structure of DNA in 1953.
The arc from X-ray crystallography to the double
helix was direct — Dorothy Hodgkin, Max Perutz, and
Rosalind Franklin all used techniques that descended
from the Braggs' original methods.
