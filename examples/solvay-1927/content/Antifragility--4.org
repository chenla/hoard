** Scope Note

Nassim Taleb's term for systems that gain from disorder
— not merely robust (surviving shocks) or resilient
(recovering from them), but actively strengthened by
volatility, stress, and uncertainty.  The opposite of
fragile is not robust; it is antifragile.

** Notes

Taleb introduced the concept in /Antifragile: Things
That Gain from Disorder/ (2012), arguing that the
category had been unnamed despite being ubiquitous.
Evolution is antifragile: random mutation plus
selection pressure produces adaptation.  Muscles are
antifragile: stress makes them stronger.  Restaurant
ecosystems are antifragile: individual failures improve
the collective by removing the weakest.

The key distinction from resilience: a resilient system
returns to its prior state after a shock.  An
antifragile system moves to a better state.  This
requires optionality — the ability to benefit from
upside while limiting downside — and it requires
exposure to stressors at the right scale.

Scale matters critically.  A system can be antifragile
at one level precisely because its components are
fragile at a lower level.  The restaurant ecosystem
improves because individual restaurants can fail.
Evolution works because individual organisms die.
This is why Taleb argues against bailouts and
centralized risk management: they remove the small
failures that keep the system healthy, guaranteeing
larger, systemic failures later.

For Hoard, antifragility is an architectural goal.
Rules-based harnesses are fragile — they break when
conditions change.  Homoiconic structures are
antifragile — they regenerate from the data, and
changes in the data produce a better-adapted harness.
The hord improves through use because each card added,
each relationship captured, each expression refined
makes the system's world-model more accurate.
