** Scope Note

French-American physicist known for Brillouin zones in solid-state physics and the WKB approximation in quantum mechanics. Later connected thermodynamic entropy to information theory. His work bridged quantum mechanics, crystallography, and the physics of information.

** Notes

Léon Brillouin (1889–1969).  French-American
physicist.  Son of the physicist Marcel Brillouin.
Known for Brillouin zones in solid-state physics,
which describe the fundamental domains of reciprocal
lattice space in crystals — essential to the theory
of electronic band structure.

Co-developed the WKB (Wentzel-Kramers-Brillouin)
semiclassical approximation method for solving
quantum mechanical problems.  Later made significant
contributions to information theory, connecting
thermodynamic entropy to information through his
negentropy principle — the idea that information is
equivalent to negative entropy.  This bridged
physics and information theory in ways that
anticipated much later developments.

Emigrated to the United States during World War II
and remained there, working at Columbia and IBM.
His career arc — from quantum mechanics in Paris to
solid-state physics to information theory in
America — reflected the broader migration of
European physics across the Atlantic.

*** Key Works

**** Before Solvay

- /Brillouin zones/ (1926).  Defined the fundamental
  domains of reciprocal space in crystals —
  essential to electronic band structure theory.

**** After Solvay

- /Science and Information Theory/ (1956).  Major
  work connecting thermodynamic entropy to
  information, establishing the negentropy
  principle.

- /Wave Propagation in Periodic Structures/ (1946).
  Foundational text on wave behavior in crystals
  and lattice structures.
