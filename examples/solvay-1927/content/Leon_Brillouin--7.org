** Notes

Léon Brillouin (1889–1969).  French-American
physicist.  Son of the physicist Marcel Brillouin.
Known for Brillouin zones in solid-state physics,
which describe the fundamental domains of reciprocal
lattice space in crystals — essential to the theory
of electronic band structure.

Co-developed the WKB (Wentzel-Kramers-Brillouin)
semiclassical approximation method for solving
quantum mechanical problems.  Later made significant
contributions to information theory, connecting
thermodynamic entropy to information (Brillouin's
negentropy principle).  Emigrated to the United
States during World War II and remained there,
working at Columbia and IBM.
