** Scope Note

Russell and Whitehead's monumental 1910–1913 attempt to
derive all of mathematics from logic.  Three volumes,
362 pages to prove 1+1=2.  The greatest formal system
ever built — and the monument Gödel toppled.

** Notes

Alfred North Whitehead and Bertrand Russell spent over a
decade constructing /Principia Mathematica/ to rescue
mathematics from the paradoxes that Cantor's work had
exposed — including Russell's own paradox, which showed
that naive set theory was inconsistent.  The three
volumes (1910, 1912, 1913) attempted to derive all
mathematical truths from a small set of logical axioms
using formal rules of inference.

The work assumed that a complete, consistent foundation
for mathematics was achievable — that logic could serve
as the bedrock on which the entire structure rested.
Gödel's incompleteness theorems showed it was not.  Any
system as powerful as /Principia/ must contain true
statements it cannot prove.

The irony is deep: Whitehead later developed process
philosophy, which embraced exactly the kind of
indeterminacy and flux that /Principia/ had tried to
eliminate.  Russell moved to philosophy of language and
political activism.  Neither returned to the foundational
project.  The work remains a monument — to ambition, to
rigor, and to the limits of formalization.
