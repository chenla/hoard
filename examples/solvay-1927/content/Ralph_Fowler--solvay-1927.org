** Notes

October 1927.  Fowler is 38.  He is Dirac's doctoral
supervisor — the man who introduced the quiet young
engineer from Bristol to quantum mechanics.  He is
also Rutherford's son-in-law, having married Eileen
Rutherford in 1921.

Fowler is the bridge between British physics and the
continental quantum revolution.  Cambridge has the
Cavendish Laboratory, the greatest experimental
physics laboratory in the world, but it has been
slower to embrace quantum theory than Copenhagen or
Göttingen.  Fowler is the one who brings the new
ideas across the Channel, who reads the German papers
and explains them to his students.

He will supervise 64 doctoral students in total —
an extraordinary number.  Dirac, sitting near him in
the middle row, is the most brilliant of them, but
far from the only one who owes his career to Fowler's
guidance and connections.

His wife Eileen will die in 1930.  Fowler himself
will die in 1944, at 55 — too young, and before
many of the physicists he trained have reached the
peak of their influence.  In October 1927 he is the
connector, the enabler, the man who makes it possible
for a Bristol engineer's son to sit among the
greatest physicists in the world.
