** Notes

Jules-Émile Verschaffelt (1870–1955).  Belgian
experimental physicist.  Worked under Heike
Kamerlingh Onnes at the cryogenics laboratory in
Leiden before returning to Belgium as professor at
the University of Ghent.

Served as the principal organizer and secretary of
the Solvay Conferences for many years, handling the
logistical and administrative work that made the
meetings run.  While his own research in experimental
thermodynamics and critical phenomena was competent,
his lasting contribution was institutional — ensuring
the Solvay Conferences brought the right people
together at the right time.

The Leiden connection was formative.  Kamerlingh Onnes
ran the world's leading low-temperature physics
laboratory, the place that first liquefied helium in
1908 and where the experimental infrastructure of
20th-century physics was being built.  Verschaffelt
trained there, absorbed Onnes's obsessive standards
for experimental precision, and carried those
standards into his organizational work.  A conference
is also an experiment: you are testing whether the
right conditions will produce something new.
Verschaffelt applied the same discipline.

As secretary of the Solvay Conferences from 1911
onward, Verschaffelt handled the logistics that made
the science possible — correspondence with attendees
in multiple languages, preparation and distribution
of reports in advance, minutes of the discussions,
coordination with the Belgian industrialist Ernest
Solvay who funded the whole enterprise.  The 1927
conference, the one that produced the photograph and
the debate, was his work as much as Lorentz's
chairmanship or Bohr's ideas.  The people who make
the room possible rarely appear in histories of what
happened in the room.
