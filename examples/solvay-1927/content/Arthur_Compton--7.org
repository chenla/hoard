** Notes

Arthur Compton (1892–1962).  American physicist.
Nobel Prize in Physics 1927 (shared with C.T.R.
Wilson) for discovering the Compton effect — the
increase in wavelength of X-rays when scattered by
electrons — which provided definitive evidence for
the particle nature of electromagnetic radiation.

Professor at the University of Chicago, he later
played a key role in the Manhattan Project, directing
the Metallurgical Laboratory where the first
controlled nuclear chain reaction was achieved under
Enrico Fermi in December 1942.
