** Scope Note

The compiled relationship unit in Hoard: a four-part
tuple of subject, predicate, object, and context.
Cards are source code; quads are the compiled output.
Every relationship in the system — identity, hierarchy,
association, membership, ordering — is expressed as a
quad stored in a TSV file.

** Notes

The quad format extends the semantic web's triple
(subject-predicate-object) with a fourth element:
context.  Context answers "according to whom?" or
"in what situation?" — the dimension that triples
lack and that makes them insufficient for real-world
knowledge.

A quad like:

  Einstein  v:rt  Determinism  solvay-1927

says: Einstein is related to Determinism, in the
context of the Solvay 1927 holon.  The same Einstein
in a different holon might have different relationships
foregrounded.  Context makes this possible without
contradiction.

Quads are compiled from cards by the hord compiler
(hord compile).  The compiler reads all card files,
extracts relations, scope notes, tags, and holon
membership, and writes the results to .hord/quads.tsv.
This is a two-pass process: pass 1 compiles all cards;
pass 2 resolves holon membership.

Quads are stored as plain TSV — no database, no binary
format.  They can be grepped, sorted, diffed, and
version-controlled like any other text file.  This is
a deliberate design choice: the compiled output must
be as transparent as the source.

Predicates come from the vocabulary.  Each predicate
has a defined meaning and belongs to a specific overlay
(Strata or Structural).  The vocabulary constrains
what quads can say, ensuring that relationships are
meaningful rather than arbitrary.
