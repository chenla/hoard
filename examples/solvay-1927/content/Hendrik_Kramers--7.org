** Notes

Hendrik Kramers (1894–1952).  Dutch physicist.  Bohr's
first and most important assistant at the Institute
for Theoretical Physics in Copenhagen.  Co-developed
the Kramers-Heisenberg dispersion formula, which was
a crucial stepping stone toward matrix mechanics.

Contributed to the WKB (Wentzel-Kramers-Brillouin)
approximation method in quantum mechanics and to the
Kramers-Kronig relations in optics.  One of the most
technically skilled physicists of his generation, yet
often overshadowed by his more famous collaborators
— Bohr, Heisenberg, Pauli all relied on his
mathematical abilities but received more credit.

He succeeded Paul Ehrenfest as professor at Leiden
in 1934, under tragic circumstances — Ehrenfest had
taken his own life the previous year.  Kramers
carried the Leiden tradition forward with distinction
until his own early death at 57.

*** Key Works

**** Before Solvay

- /Kramers-Heisenberg dispersion formula/ (1925).
  Crucial stepping stone toward Heisenberg's matrix
  mechanics — described how light is scattered by
  atoms.

**** After Solvay

- /Kramers-Kronig relations/ (1927–1929).  Connected
  the real and imaginary parts of optical response
  functions — still fundamental to spectroscopy and
  signal processing.
