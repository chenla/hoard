** Notes

Max Planck (1858–1947).  German theoretical physicist.
Nobel Prize in Physics 1918 for his discovery of energy
quanta.  In 1900 he introduced the quantum of action
(Planck's constant, h), resolving the black-body
radiation problem and inadvertently launching the
quantum revolution.

Professor at the University of Berlin for decades, he
became the elder statesman of German physics.  At the
1927 Solvay Conference he watched the young generation
debate the meaning of the theory his work had made
possible.  He remained philosophically conservative,
never fully comfortable with the Copenhagen
interpretation.
