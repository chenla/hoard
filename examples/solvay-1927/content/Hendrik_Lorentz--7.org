** Notes

Hendrik Lorentz (1853–1928).  Dutch theoretical
physicist.  Nobel Prize in Physics 1902 (shared with
Pieter Zeeman) for work on electromagnetic radiation
and the Zeeman effect.  Developed the Lorentz
transformations, which became the mathematical
foundation of Einstein's special relativity.

Served as chairman of every Solvay Conference from the
first in 1911 until his death.  The 1927 conference
was his last — he died four months later in February
1928.  Universally respected, he was one of the few
figures trusted by all sides in the emerging quantum
debates.

Lorentz's authority rested on more than seniority.  His
electron theory of the 1890s had been the most advanced
attempt to unify electromagnetism and matter before
relativity, and his Lorentz transformations — derived
to explain the null result of the Michelson-Morley
experiment — became the mathematical backbone of
Einstein's 1905 paper.  Einstein always acknowledged
the debt.  At the 1927 conference, when the debate
between Bohr and Einstein threatened to fracture the
proceedings, it was Lorentz's chairmanship that held the
room together.

Beyond physics, Lorentz served the Netherlands as a
public intellectual and engineer.  He led the technical
committee that calculated the hydrodynamic effects of
closing the Zuiderzee — a massive water management
project that required modeling tidal flows across an
entire inland sea.  It was applied physics at
civilizational scale, and Lorentz treated it with the
same rigor he brought to electromagnetic theory.

His funeral in February 1928 was a state occasion.
Telegraph services across the Netherlands were suspended
for three minutes as a mark of respect — an honor
normally reserved for heads of state.  Einstein delivered
a eulogy, calling him "the greatest and noblest man of
our times."

*** Key Works

**** Before Solvay

- Electron theory of metals (1892–1895).  Unified
  electromagnetic and optical phenomena through a
  theory of microscopic charged particles.
- Lorentz transformations (1899–1904).  The coordinate
  transformations that became the mathematical
  foundation of special relativity.
- Nobel Prize in Physics (1902).  Shared with Pieter
  Zeeman for research on the influence of magnetism
  on radiation.

**** After Solvay

- Zuiderzee hydrodynamic calculations (1918–1926).
  Led the committee modeling tidal effects of enclosing
  the Zuiderzee, a major Dutch engineering project.
- Chairman, Solvay Conferences (1911–1927).  Presided
  over every conference from the first through the
  fifth, providing continuity and diplomatic skill
  across the most turbulent period in physics.
