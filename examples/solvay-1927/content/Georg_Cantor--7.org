** Scope Note

German mathematician (1845–1918).  Created set theory
and proved the existence of multiple infinities — the
first demonstration that the mathematical universe was
stranger and wilder than anyone had imagined.  Opposed
by Kronecker, defended by Hilbert.  Suffered recurring
mental illness.

** Notes

Georg Ferdinand Ludwig Philipp Cantor (1845–1918).  Born
in Saint Petersburg to a German-Danish family, spent his
entire academic career at the University of Halle — a
provincial institution far from the major mathematical
centers.

His diagonal argument (1891) proved the real numbers are
uncountable — there are strictly more real numbers than
natural numbers, even though both sets are infinite.
This was the first proof that infinity comes in
different sizes, and it shattered the assumption that
infinity was a single, manageable concept.

Leopold Kronecker, one of the most powerful
mathematicians in Germany, attacked Cantor's work as
mathematical pathology and actively blocked his
advancement.  "God made the integers; all else is the
work of man," Kronecker declared.  Hilbert defended
Cantor, calling set theory "the finest product of
mathematical genius" and "a paradise from which no one
shall drive us."

Cantor suffered recurring bouts of severe depression,
was hospitalized multiple times, and spent his final
years in a sanatorium.  He died in 1918, impoverished,
during the last winter of the Great War.
