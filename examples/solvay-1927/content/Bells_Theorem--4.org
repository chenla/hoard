** Scope Note

John Bell's 1964 theorem proving that no local
hidden-variable theory can reproduce all predictions of
quantum mechanics.  Transformed the Bohr-Einstein debate
from philosophy into experimental physics — and the
experiments sided with Bohr.

** Notes

Bell derived an inequality that any local realist
theory must satisfy.  The inequality sets an upper bound
on the correlations between measurements on entangled
particles under the assumption that (a) particles have
definite properties before measurement (realism) and
(b) measurement of one particle cannot instantaneously
affect a distant particle (locality).

Quantum mechanics predicts violations of this inequality
for certain entangled states.  Alain Aspect's 1982
experiments confirmed the violation with high
statistical significance.  Subsequent experiments have
closed progressively more loopholes — the 2015 Delft
experiment was the first to close all major loopholes
simultaneously.

The result is stark: either locality is false (particles
can be correlated instantaneously regardless of
distance) or realism is false (particles do not have
definite properties before measurement), or both.  The
question Einstein raised at breakfast at Solvay in 1927
was finally answered experimentally — and the answer was
not what Einstein hoped.

Bell's day job was designing particle accelerators at
CERN.  The foundations work was done in his spare time,
almost as a private obsession — he called quantum
foundations "my hobby."  This marginality is itself
telling: in the decades after Copenhagen's victory,
questioning the interpretation of quantum mechanics was
considered disreputable, and Bell's theorem was largely
ignored by mainstream physics for years after
publication.

It took eighteen years from theorem (1964) to
definitive experiment (1982).  The delay was partly
technological — Aspect needed single-atom cascade
sources and fast polarization switches that did not
exist in the 1960s — and partly sociological.  The
physics community did not consider the question
important.  Aspect's experiments at Orsay changed that,
but even then the full recognition was slow.  The 2022
Nobel Prize in Physics, awarded to Alain Aspect, John
Clauser, and Anton Zeilinger "for experiments with
entangled photons, establishing the violation of Bell
inequalities and pioneering quantum information
science," came fifty-eight years after the theorem and
forty years after the key experiments.  Bell himself
died in 1990, never receiving the prize his work made
possible.

The result means that nature is either nonlocal or
non-real — either way, the classical worldview is dead.
There is no interpretation of the experimental data
that preserves both local causality and pre-existing
definite properties.  The universe, at its most
fundamental level, is not the clockwork that Newton and
Laplace described.
