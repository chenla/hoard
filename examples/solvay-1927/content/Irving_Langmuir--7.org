** Notes

Irving Langmuir (1881–1957).  American chemist and
physicist.  Nobel Prize in Chemistry 1932 for
discoveries in surface chemistry — particularly the
Langmuir adsorption isotherm, which describes the
relationship between gas pressure and the amount
adsorbed on a surface.

Spent most of his career at the General Electric
Research Laboratory in Schenectady, New York, where
he developed the gas-filled incandescent lamp and
made fundamental contributions to plasma physics
(he coined the term "plasma").  Also pioneered cloud
seeding for weather modification.  One of the few
industrial scientists to win a Nobel Prize and to
attend the Solvay Conferences.
