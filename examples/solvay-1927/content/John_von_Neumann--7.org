** Scope Note

Hungarian-American mathematician (1903–1957).  Polymath
who formalized quantum mechanics, co-designed the hydrogen
bomb, invented game theory, created the architecture for
stored-program computers, and laid the foundations for
cellular automata and artificial life.

** Notes

John von Neumann (1903–1957).  Born János Lajos Neumann
in Budapest.  PhD in mathematics from the University of
Budapest at 22, simultaneously earning a diploma in
chemical engineering from ETH Zurich.

By the time of the Fifth Solvay Conference he had already
published his mathematical formalization of quantum
mechanics.  He proved the impossibility of hidden
variables in quantum theory — a result that stood for
decades until John Bell showed it rested on an
assumption von Neumann himself would not have defended.

He recognized Gödel's incompleteness theorems immediately
and proved the complementary result: a complete
mathematical system is necessarily inconsistent.

At the Institute for Advanced Study in Princeton he
designed the IAS machine — he called it the MANIAC
(Mathematical Analyzer, Numerical Integrator, and
Automatic Computer).  Its stored-program architecture
became the template for virtually all subsequent
computers.

Along the way he helped design the implosion lens for
the plutonium bomb, invented game theory (with Oskar
Morgenstern), created the theory of self-reproducing
automata, and wrote the first serious treatment of
the economics of computation.

On his deathbed in 1957, ravaged by cancer likely caused
by radiation exposure at nuclear tests, he whispered that
a thinking machine "would have to grow, not be built...
understand language... and play, like a child."  He died
at 53, leaving behind work that would take decades to
fully absorb.

*** Key Works

**** Before Solvay

- /Zur Theorie der Gesellschaftsspiele/ (1928).  The
  minimax theorem — founding paper of game theory,
  proving that every finite two-person zero-sum game
  has an optimal strategy.
- /Mathematische Grundlagen der Quantenmechanik/ (1932).
  Mathematical Foundations of Quantum Mechanics — the
  rigorous Hilbert-space formalization that dominated
  quantum theory for decades, including the (flawed)
  proof of the impossibility of hidden variables.

**** After Solvay

- /First Draft of a Report on the EDVAC/ (1945).  The
  stored-program architecture that became the template
  for virtually all subsequent computers.
- MANIAC computer (1951).  The IAS machine — designed
  at Princeton, used for hydrogen bomb calculations
  and some of the first numerical weather predictions.
- Hydrogen bomb implosion lens (1940s).  Co-designed
  the shaped-charge geometry for the plutonium bomb.
- /Theory of Self-Reproducing Automata/ (posthumous,
  1966).  Cellular automata and the theory of
  machines that can build copies of themselves —
  anticipating both artificial life and the logic of
  biological reproduction.
- /The Computer and the Brain/ (posthumous, 1958).
  Unfinished lectures comparing neural architecture
  to digital computation — where the biological turn
  was heading when he died.
