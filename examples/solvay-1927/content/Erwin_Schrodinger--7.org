** Scope Note

Austrian physicist (1887–1961). Nobel Prize 1933. Formulated the wave equation (1926) that gave quantum mechanics its continuous mathematical framework. Sided with Einstein against Copenhagen. Later devised the cat thought experiment to expose what he saw as the absurdity of quantum superposition taken literally.

** Notes

Erwin Schrödinger (1887–1961).  Austrian-Irish
physicist.  Nobel Prize in Physics 1933 (shared with
Dirac) for the development of wave mechanics.  In
1926 he published the Schrödinger equation, which
describes how the quantum state of a physical system
changes over time — the foundational equation of
non-relativistic quantum mechanics.

His wave mechanics provided an alternative to
Heisenberg's matrix mechanics and was shown to be
mathematically equivalent.  Later proposed the famous
Schrödinger's cat thought experiment (1935) to
illustrate what he saw as absurdities in the
Copenhagen interpretation.  Fled Austria after the
Anschluss and eventually settled at the Dublin
Institute for Advanced Studies.

At the 1927 Solvay Conference, Schrödinger was aligned
with Einstein and de Broglie against the Copenhagen
camp.  He found the probabilistic interpretation of his
own equation deeply unsatisfying — he had intended the
wave function to describe something real and continuous,
not a probability amplitude.  Born's probabilistic
reinterpretation of his wave function was, in
Schrödinger's view, a betrayal of the physics.  "I
don't like it," he told Bohr, "and I'm sorry I ever
had anything to do with it."

His later career took unexpected turns.  In Dublin,
sheltered by Éamon de Valera at the Institute for
Advanced Studies, he wrote /What is Life?/ (1944) — a
slim book that asked what physics could say about
biological processes.  His speculation that genetic
information might be stored in an "aperiodic crystal"
directly inspired Watson and Crick's search for the
structure of DNA.  A physicist's side project became
one of the founding documents of molecular biology.

Schrödinger returned to Vienna in 1956 and died there
in 1961.  His nationality shifted three times —
Austrian, then Irish by necessity, then Austrian again.
His wave equation remains the foundational equation of
non-relativistic quantum mechanics, used daily by every
working physicist, even as the interpretation he
preferred for it remains a minority position.

*** Key Works

**** Before Solvay

- Schrödinger equation (1926).  The foundational
  equation of wave mechanics, describing how quantum
  states evolve in time.
- Proof of equivalence of wave and matrix mechanics
  (1926).  Showed that his wave mechanics and
  Heisenberg's matrix mechanics were mathematically
  equivalent formulations.

**** After Solvay

- Schrödinger's cat thought experiment (1935).
  Designed to expose the absurdity of superposition
  applied to macroscopic objects.
- /What is Life?/ (1944).  Speculated on the physical
  basis of genetics; inspired Watson and Crick's
  discovery of the DNA double helix.
- Nobel Prize in Physics (1933).  Shared with Dirac
  for the discovery of new productive forms of atomic
  theory.
