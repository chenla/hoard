** Notes

Erwin Schrödinger (1887–1961).  Austrian-Irish
physicist.  Nobel Prize in Physics 1933 (shared with
Dirac) for the development of wave mechanics.  In
1926 he published the Schrödinger equation, which
describes how the quantum state of a physical system
changes over time — the foundational equation of
non-relativistic quantum mechanics.

His wave mechanics provided an alternative to
Heisenberg's matrix mechanics and was shown to be
mathematically equivalent.  Later proposed the famous
Schrödinger's cat thought experiment (1935) to
illustrate what he saw as absurdities in the
Copenhagen interpretation.  Fled Austria after the
Anschluss and eventually settled at the Dublin
Institute for Advanced Studies.
