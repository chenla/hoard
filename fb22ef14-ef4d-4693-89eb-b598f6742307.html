Notes

October 1927. Kramers is 32. He has spent nearly a decade as Bohr's chief assistant in Copenhagen — longer than anyone. He co-developed the Kramers-Heisenberg dispersion formula with the young Heisenberg in 1925, a key bridge between the old and new quantum theory.

He is the man closest to Bohr's thinking, the one who has absorbed the Copenhagen spirit most deeply. When Bohr presents complementarity, Kramers understands it better than almost anyone in the room. The "K" in the WKB approximation is his.

But there is a cost to being Bohr's lieutenant for so long. Kramers has his own ideas — his own contributions to quantum mechanics are substantial — yet he is often overshadowed by the more dramatic figures around him. Heisenberg, five years younger, has already surpassed him in reputation.

He will eventually leave Copenhagen, succeed Ehrenfest at Leiden in 1934, and build his own legacy. But in October 1927 he stands with Bohr, the loyal collaborator, watching the framework they helped build transform physics.
